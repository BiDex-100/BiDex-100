BiDex-100

A Comprehensive Benchmark for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation Across 100+ Simulation Tasks

Paper Supplementary Code Dataset Assets Leaderboard

Abstract

Bimanual manipulation with dexterous hands has attracted growing attention in research on robotic action models. However, few studies have developed benchmarks specifically tailored for dexterous manipulation, leaving it unclear how the dexterous-manipulation capability of existing models actually bears out. We present BiDex-100, a simulation benchmark for bimanual dexterous manipulation that combines broad task coverage, multi-dimensional evaluation, and high-DoF hand embodiments. Concretely, the benchmark comprises 106 tasks organized along five capability axes: Generalization, Instruction Following, Limited Demonstrations, Long Horizon, and Memory. Each task is paired with an out-of-domain variant featuring controlled visual and spatial changes, allowing systematic assessment of robustness. In addition, the benchmark supports two embodiments and provides 8,080 success-validated teleoperated demonstrations, totaling 6.25M frames. We evaluate seven recent Vision-Language-Action models under a unified protocol, establishing baselines for capability-specific performance, robustness, and data efficiency. Code, tasks, data, and evaluation tools will be made public.

Overview

Overview of BiDex-100. The benchmark provides 106 declarative task specifications across five capability axes, 8,080 success-validated teleoperated demonstrations on two bimanual embodiments equipped with dexterous hands, and progress-based scoring under matched In-Domain and Out-of-Domain protocols. Five representative tasks, one per capability axis — Memory, Long-Horizon, Instruction-Following, Limited-Demonstrations, and Generalization — each shown with a real demonstration and its language instruction.

Five Capability Axes

The suite design turns “is the policy good?” into “what is the policy good at?” Results form a capability profile rather than a single aggregate score.

Generalization
40 tasks
Single- and few-stage skills — lifting, placement, insertion, threading, and cooking — over a library of 300 object assets, including 10 tasks on the Astribot S1 morphology. Measures how skills transfer across object geometry and embodiment.
Instruction-Following
20 tasks
Verbal-command fidelity tested three ways: count variants (one vs.\ two plants/nails), combo tasks conjoining two instructions, and switch tasks that replace the instruction mid-episode. Wrong-object grasps terminate the episode.
Limited-Demonstrations
26 tasks
Data-scarce skill acquisition: every task ships with only 30 demonstrations. Includes 11 articraft_open_* tasks — microwaves, cash registers, rice cookers — requiring force-aware actuation of hinged, sliding, and rotary joints.
Long-Horizon
10 tasks
Multi-stage chores — barbecue grilling, fridge restocking, dessert plating, recycling sorting — with 4–9 subtasks, 6.5 objects per scene, and demonstration durations averaging 50 s (max 97 s). Success requires sustaining a plan far beyond the policy's action chunk.
Memory
10 tasks
Latent-state tracking under perceptual aliasing: recolor cubes hidden behind a button sequence, recall FIFO basket-removal order, and remember targets shown only in a reveal phase. These probe genuine temporal state tracking rather than reactive control.
106 tasks · 5 axes
graded · ID/OOD
Across the 106 specifications, tasks carry 1–10 subtasks (mean 3.6) and compose 472 predicate instances. Each ships with a baked __ood variant with identical seed and initial state, so every policy sees identical out-of-domain conditions.

Beyond the headline numbers, the benchmark supports three analyses that binary, single-axis benchmarks cannot: capability profiles (whether a model's strength comes from short-horizon skills or extends to memory- and horizon-bound tasks), ID→OOD degradation (the per-axis gap under combined shifts — appearance, layout, distractor, scale, and mirroring), and data efficiency (the Limited-Demonstrations axis isolates the 30-demo regime). Progress subtask scores further distinguish “near miss” from complete failure on long-horizon tasks, where strict success rates floor near zero for every current model.
