DexSimBench

A Simulation Benchmark for 100+ Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation Tasks

Paper Code Dataset Results

Abstract

As vision-language-action (VLA) models scale to larger demonstration corpora, narrow pick-and-place benchmarks are becoming less informative. Progress now depends on whether models can handle capability limits such as memory, long-horizon control, changing instructions, few-shot learning, and visual shifts. These limits are especially important for bimanual dexterous manipulation, where two multi-fingered hands must coordinate contact-rich actions beyond the scope of parallel-jaw settings. We present DexSimBench, a simulation benchmark for measuring these capabilities in bimanual dexterous manipulation. It includes 107 tasks across five axes: Generalization, Instruction-Following, Limited Demonstrations, Long Horizon, and Memory. Tasks are defined through compositional goals built from typed predicates, enabling graded evaluation that identifies partial progress and specific failure modes. Each task also has an out-of-domain variant with controlled visual and spatial changes, allowing direct measurement of robustness. DexSimBench supports two 22-DoF hand embodiments and provides 8,110 success-validated teleoperated demonstrations, totaling 6.25M frames and 69.5 hours. We evaluate seven recent vision-language-action models under a unified protocol, establishing baselines for capability-specific performance, robustness, and data efficiency. Code, tasks, data, and evaluation tools are publicly available.

Overview

DexSimBench overview. The benchmark provides 107 declarative task specifications across five capability axes, 8,110 success-validated teleoperated demonstrations on two bimanual embodiments equipped with dexterous hands, and graded predicate-based scoring under matched In-Domain and Out-of-Domain protocols. Five representative tasks, one per capability axis, are shown through frames of a real demonstration with its language instruction: Memory, Long-Horizon, Instruction-Following, Limited-Demonstrations, and Generalization.

Five Capability Axes

The suite design turns “is the policy good?” into “what is the policy good at?” Results form a capability profile rather than a single aggregate score.

Generalization
40 tasks
Single- and few-stage skills — lifting, placement, insertion, threading, and cooking — over a library of 300 object assets, including 10 tasks on the Astribot S1 morphology. Measures how skills transfer across object geometry and embodiment.
Instruction-Following
20 tasks
Verbal-command fidelity tested three ways: count variants (one vs.\ two plants/nails), combo tasks conjoining two instructions, and switch tasks that replace the instruction mid-episode. Wrong-object grasps terminate the episode.
Limited-Demonstrations
27 tasks
Data-scarce skill acquisition: every task ships with only 30 demonstrations. Includes 11 articraft_open_* tasks — microwaves, cash registers, rice cookers — requiring force-aware actuation of hinged, sliding, and rotary joints.
Long-Horizon
10 tasks
Multi-stage chores — barbecue grilling, fridge restocking, dessert plating, recycling sorting — with 4–9 subtasks, 6.5 objects per scene, and demonstration durations averaging 50 s (max 97 s). Success requires sustaining a plan far beyond the policy's action chunk.
Memory
10 tasks
Latent-state tracking under perceptual aliasing: recolor cubes hidden behind a button sequence, recall FIFO basket-removal order, and remember targets shown only in a reveal phase. These probe genuine temporal state tracking rather than reactive control.
107 tasks · 5 axes
graded · ID/OOD
Across the 107 specifications, tasks carry 1–10 subtasks (mean 3.6) and compose 472 predicate instances. Each ships with a baked __ood variant with identical seed and initial state, so every policy sees identical out-of-domain conditions.

Experiments

We benchmark seven recent VLA models under a unified protocol: 58-DoF joint control, the same all-category data mixture, 30k training steps on 8×H20 GPUs, and 3 seeds × 10 episodes per task per domain (6,420 rollouts per model). Graded scores reward partial progress; success requires a perfect 100.

Beyond the headline numbers, the benchmark supports three analyses that binary, single-axis benchmarks cannot: capability profiles (whether a model's strength comes from short-horizon skills or extends to memory- and horizon-bound tasks), ID→OOD degradation (localizing robustness failures to texture/lighting shift, layout offset, distractor interference, or mirroring), and data efficiency (the Limited-Demonstrations axis isolates the 30-demo regime). Graded subtask scores further distinguish “near miss” from complete failure on long-horizon tasks, where strict success rates are expected to floor near zero for all current models.
